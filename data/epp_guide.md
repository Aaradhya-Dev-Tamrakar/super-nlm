# Engineering Professional Practice Core Foundations & Legal Trap Guide
## CE 752: Engineering Professional Practice (40 Marks Theory / 10 Assessment / 50 Total)

This reference guide details legal statutes, ethical frameworks, FIDIC contract administration principles, tendering procedures, and exam case study mechanics for CE 752.

---

### Unit 1: History of Engineering Practices (3 Hours — ~4-5 Marks)

#### 1. Historical Epochs
- **Eastern Engineering**:
  - Vedic/Ancient civilization public works: Indus Valley (sanitation, grid towns, brick masonry), ancient canals and irrigation (Raj Kulo in Nepal), Newar architecture and traditional water spouts (Dhunge Dhara / Hiti systems), pagoda and stupa construction engineering.
  - Ethical philosophy: *Dharma* as duty towards community and preservation of natural elements (*Panchabhuta*).
- **Western Engineering**:
  - Pre-industrial master-builders (Roman aqueducts, arches, roads, Vitruvian principles: *firmitas, utilitas, venustas*).
  - Industrial Revolution (18th-19th Century): Steam engine, mechanization, rise of formalized engineering institutions (Institution of Civil Engineers 1818).
- **Engineering Practices in Nepal**:
  - Pre-2007 BS: Traditional craftsmanship under Ranas (Chandra Canal, Pharping Hydro 1911 AD, Bhimphedi ropeway).
  - Post-2007 BS: Department of Roads (DoR), Department of Electricity, establishment of Institute of Engineering (Pulchowk 1972 AD).
  - Institutional regulation: Nepal Engineering Council (NEC) formed under NEC Act 2055 (1998 AD).

### Unit 2: Profession and Ethics (6 Hours — ~8-10 Marks)

#### 1. Characteristics of a True Profession
- Specialized, systematically acquired theoretical knowledge and advanced skills.
- Formal accreditation and licensing by statutory governing bodies (NEC).
- Self-regulation and commitment to public welfare over commercial profit (*Altruism*).
- Adherence to an enforceable Code of Conduct.

#### 2. Professional Institutions & NEC Code of Ethics
- **Nepal Engineering Council (NEC)**: Statutory registration body (Category A: General, Category B: Non-degree/experience, Category C: Foreign, and newer Master/Professional designations).
- **Nepal Engineers' Association (NEA)**: Voluntary professional society focused on professional development and advocacy.
- **NEC 7 Fundamental Codes of Conduct**:
  1. Priority to public safety, health, and environmental welfare.
  2. Perform services only in areas of competence.
  3. Issue public statements only in an objective and truthful manner.
  4. Act for each employer or client as faithful agents or trustees (Confidentiality).
  5. Avoid deceptive acts and conflicts of interest.
  6. Conduct oneself honorably, responsibly, and lawfully to enhance professional dignity.
  7. Continuous professional development and mentorship of junior engineers.

#### 3. Legal Liability & Negligence
- **Tort Liability vs Contractual Liability**:
  - *Contractual Liability*: Breach of explicit promises or implied terms set forth in a written agreement between contracting parties.
  - *Tort Liability (Civil Wrong / Law of Torts)*: Breach of a general legal duty of care owed to any person who can foreseeably be injured by the engineer's work (neighbor principle from *Donoghue v Stevenson*).
- **Elements to Prove Professional Negligence**:
  1. **Duty of Care**: The engineer owed a duty of care to the claimant.
  2. **Breach of Duty**: Standard of work fell below what a reasonably competent engineer would do (*Bolam test*).
  3. **Causation**: The breach directly caused the damage/loss (*"but-for"* test).
  4. **Remoteness / Foreseeability**: The damage was reasonably foreseeable and not too remote.
- **Vicarious Liability**: An employer/firm is held legally liable for the tortious acts of employees committed within the course of employment.

### Unit 3: Professional Practices in Nepal (3 Hours — ~4-5 Marks)

#### 1. Public vs Private Sector Dynamics
- **Public Sector (Civil Service / GoN / Public Enterprises)**:
  - Governed by Civil Service Act (*Nijamati Sewa Ain*), Public Procurement Act 2063, Financial Administration Rules.
  - Hiring via Public Service Commission (*Lok Sewa Aayog*).
  - Characteristics: Fixed hierarchies, job security, strict procedural compliance, bureaucratic delays.
- **Private Sector (Consultancy, Construction Contracting, Telecom Vendors)**:
  - Governed by Company Act 2063, Labor Act 2074.
  - Characteristics: Performance-based incentives, rapid technology adoption, financial volatility, requirement for professional indemnity insurance.

### Unit 4: Contract Management & Procurement (6 Hours — ~10-12 Marks)

#### 1. The Legal Anatomy of a Valid Contract (Contract Act 2056)
- **Essential Elements**:
  1. Offer and Unconditional Acceptance.
  2. Free Consent (Absence of Coercion, Undue Influence, Fraud, Misrepresentation, Mutual Mistake).
  3. Lawful Consideration.
  4. Legal Capacity of Parties (Sound mind, majority age).
  5. Lawful Object / Purpose.
  6. Intention to create legal relations.

#### 2. Tendering & Procurement Cycle (Public Procurement Act 2063 / PPR 2064)
- **Procurement Methods**:
  - **Open Competitive Bidding (NCB - National / ICB - International)**: Mandatory for major public works.
  - **Sealed Quotation**: For smaller thresholds as per PPR rules.
  - **Direct Procurement**: Proprietary items, emergencies.
  - **Community / User Committee Work**: Limited thresholds for local rural development.
- **Key Tender Securities / Guarantees**:
  - **Bid Security (Earnest Money)**: Typically $2\%\text{--}3\%$ of estimated cost; valid for 30 days beyond bid validity period. Forfeited if bidder withdraws during validity or refuses to sign contract.
  - **Performance Security**: Typically $5\%$ of contract price (increased if bid is substantially below estimate: front-loading penalty); ensures faithful execution.
  - **Advance Payment Guarantee (APG)**: Mobilization advance (usually up to $10\%\text{--}20\%$); backed by unconditional bank guarantee, deducted incrementally from Interim Payment Certificates (IPCs).
  - **Retention Money**: $5\%$ deducted from each running bill; released $50\%$ upon Substantial Completion (Taking-Over Certificate) and remaining $50\%$ after Defect Liability Period (DLP).

#### 3. Types of Construction & Service Contracts
- **Unit Price / Item Rate Contract**: Payments based on measured quantities of completed items multiplied by BOQ unit rates. Most common in civil engineering.
- **Lump Sum Contract**: Fixed price for entire scope. Contractor absorbs quantity risk.
- **Cost Plus Contract**: Actual cost incurred plus fixed/percentage fee. Used in emergency response.
- **EPC / Turnkey (Engineering, Procurement, Construction)**: Single contractor delivers fully operational facility.

### Unit 5: Regulatory Environment (5 Hours — ~8-10 Marks)

#### 1. Key Nepalese Statutory Acts for Engineers

| Statute | Core Purpose & Critical Provisions for Engineers |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Nepal Engineering Council Act 2055** | Registration mandatory to practice engineering legally in Nepal. Classifies engineers, provides disciplinary powers to suspend/revoke licenses for misconduct. |
| **Public Procurement Act 2063 (PPA)** | Transparency, fair competition, non-discrimination in public spending. Establishes PPMO (Public Procurement Monitoring Office) and e-GP system. |
| **Labor Act 2074** | Workplace health & safety standards, working hours (8 hrs/day, 48 hrs/week), overtime pay ($1.5\times$), gratuity/social security fund (SSF), severance pay, prohibition of child labor. |
| **Company Act 2063** | Formation, registration, corporate governance, shareholder rights, Director fiduciary liabilities, liquidation of Private/Public limited entities. |
| **Copyright Act 2059 / Patent, Design & Trademark Act** | Protection of architectural drawings, software algorithms, engineering designs. Copyright exists upon creation; patent requires registration proving novelty, inventive step, industrial utility. |
| **National Building Code (NBC)** | Mandatory structural, seismic, and safety bylaws (NBC 105 for seismic design of buildings in Nepal). |

### Unit 6: Contemporary Issues & Dispute Resolution (3 Hours — ~4-5 Marks)

#### 1. Public-Private Partnerships (PPP)
- Modes: **BOOT** (Build-Own-Operate-Transfer), **BOT** (Build-Operate-Transfer), **BOO** (Build-Own-Operate).
- Used for capital-intensive infrastructure (Hydropower, toll highways). Risk allocation between private concessionaire and public authority.

#### 2. Conflict & Dispute Resolution Framework
- **Dispute Hierarchy**:
  1. **Negotiation / Amicable Settlement**: Informal discussion between project manager and contractor.
  2. **Adjudication / Dispute Board (DB / DAB)**: Independent technical panel appointed at contract start; issues binding determinations within 84 days under FIDIC.
  3. **Mediation**: Non-binding facilitation by neutral mediator.
  4. **Arbitration**: Legally binding private adjudication governed by Arbitration Act 2055 (1999 AD) / UNCITRAL rules. Faster than court, confidential, enforceable as court decree.
  5. **Litigation**: Formal court trial; public, costly, protracted.

### Unit 7: Case Studies (4 Hours — ~6-8 Marks)

#### 1. Standard Exam Analysis Methodology
Whenever an ethical or liability dilemma is presented:
1. **Identify the Primary Stakeholders** (Public, Client, Employer, Contractor, Subcontractors, Environment).
2. **Identify the Core Conflicting Duties** (Loyalty to Employer vs Public Safety / Legal Compliance).
3. **Reference Specific Codes & Acts** (NEC Code 1: Public safety paramount; Labor Act 2074 Section 68: safety measures; PPA Section 52: conflict of interest).
4. **Formulate a Defensible Professional Action Plan** (Internal escalation $\to$ Written formal objection $\to$ Refusal to seal non-compliant drawings $\to$ Whistleblowing to statutory authority if life is at risk).
